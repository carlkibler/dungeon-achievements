# DCC prompt refinement

The prompt should reproduce the achievement system's mechanics, not merely its profanity. The stable
pattern is: observe a precise trigger, make a hostile inference, take an optional tangent, then make
the reward another punchline.

## Corpus findings

The local corpus contains 149 named achievements. Its useful structural signals are:

- 66% of titles have one to three words; 90% have one to six.
- 84 rewards are named boxes. Twenty explicitly deny or negate a reward.
- Only 16 of 147 available excerpts open in a question-like form. Q&A is a trick, not a template.
- Titles use references, idioms, classifications, and double meanings more often than literal activity names.
- Early achievements explain a mechanic and insult the crawler in the same breath. Later achievements
  assume history, expose the AI's fixations, and sometimes turn against dungeon institutions.
- The stronger jokes use evidence unique to the event. Generic scores for dignity, regret, or self-respect
  sound like an achievement generator rather than the dungeon AI.

## Evaluation loop

Seven activities covered competence, deferred chores, physical effort, avoidance, forgetfulness,
grief, and elaborate procrastination. Each was generated by Claude Haiku and Codex Luna.

The original prompt forced one Q&A and one long riff in every set. It also supplied reusable reward
examples. Both models obediently exposed the template:

| Signal | Original | Final sample |
|---|---:|---:|
| Parseable sets | 14/14 | 11/11 |
| Q&A descriptions | 14/42 | 1/33 |
| Box rewards | 1/42 | 20/33 |
| Placeholder-joke hits | 12 | 0 |
| Titles within 1–6 words | 37/42 | 32/33 |
| Sets with meaningful length contrast | 11/14 | 9/11 |

The first revision fixed rewards and Q&A frequency but made descriptions uniformly clinical. A second
pass added explicit rhythm profiles. A final spot check added the missing human texture rule: at least
two descriptions must notice an object, timestamp, gesture, mess, or consequence unique to the activity.

Blind judging was split. Haiku preferred the original in six of eight pairs because its prose was more
vivid. Luna preferred the revised prompt in five of eight because it was tighter and structurally closer
to the corpus. The final prompt keeps the structural corrections while explicitly rejecting incident-report
prose and requiring concrete details. That disagreement was useful; maximizing either judge alone made the
writing worse.

## Variation design

Variation is now three independent choices before styles or the optional concrete obsession:

- Seven moods: smug delight, tutorial deadpan, fascination, hostile game-show energy, cosmic exhaustion,
  irritated admiration, and unstable fixation.
- Five snark levels: dry, two standard variants, sharp, and grudging.
- Seven set shapes with explicit rhythm ranges. Only one permits Q&A.

That gives 245 mood/snark/form combinations. Tenderness is no longer a random mood. The prompt's tender
clause overrides those random choices for grief, sobriety, illness, and similar inputs. This prevents a
mundane activity from receiving accidental greeting-card copy without pretending keyword detection is
the same thing as understanding context.

## Remaining limits

- Claude Haiku still wraps valid JSON in Markdown fences. The parser already removes them.
- Cheap models sometimes miss approximate word ranges. The ranges still improve contrast without rejecting output.
- The community corpus may contain transcription mistakes. Book locations and source URLs remain attached for checks.
